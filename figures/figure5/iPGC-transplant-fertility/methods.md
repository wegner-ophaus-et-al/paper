## Methods

### Zebrafish iPGC transplantation and fertility assessment

Induced primordial germ cells (iPGCs) were generated and transplanted into germ cell-depleted zebrafish embryos at the blastula stage, as previously described. Three experimental groups were compared: wild-type (WT) control fish (stock H213, n = 24), fish transplanted with a full mix of donor iPGCs (stocks H211, H218, H220; n = 73), and fish transplanted with iPGCs lacking *tdrd7a* (stocks H212, H219, H221; n = 76). Donor iPGCs carried a dominant red-eye transgene (Tg(*actb1:DsRed*)) enabling germline contribution tracing. At sexual maturity (3 months), individual fish were outcrossed to WT partners. Clutches were collected and scored for: (i) fertilization rate (fertilized versus unfertilized eggs), and (ii) dominant red-eye marker transmission in F1 progeny (red-eye-positive versus red-eye-negative). Fish were classified as fertile if any fertilized embryos were produced, and as dominant-marker-positive if any F1 progeny expressed the red-eye transgene. Sex was determined by examination of secondary sex characteristics and confirmed by dissection. Statistical analyses and figure generation were performed using Python (matplotlib, seaborn). Fertilization rates and dominant marker transmission rates were visualised as strip plots with median ± s.d. overlaid. Binary outcomes (fertile proportion, dominant marker proportion) were plotted as bar plots with 95% confidence intervals. Sex ratios were calculated as the percentage of males per clutch.

## Figure Legend

**Figure X. Transplantation of *tdrd7a*-deficient iPGCs impairs fertility and germline contribution in zebrafish.**

**(a)** Fertilization rates of individual fish transplanted with either full-mix iPGCs (WT donor, n = 73) or *tdrd7a*-deficient iPGCs (n = 76). Each point represents one fish; horizontal bars indicate the group median ± s.d. Many fish in both transplantation conditions failed to produce any fertilized embryos. **(b)** Germline contribution of donor-derived iPGCs, assessed as the percentage of F1 progeny carrying the dominant red-eye transgene. Each point represents one fish; median ± s.d. is shown. **(c)** Proportion of fertile fish in WT controls (n = 24), full-mix recipients (n = 73), and *tdrd7a*-deficient recipients (n = 76). Bars represent the fraction of fish that produced at least one fertilized embryo; error bars indicate 95% confidence intervals. **(d)** Proportion of transplanted fish that transmitted the donor-derived red-eye marker to F1 progeny, shown for full-mix and *tdrd7a*-deficient groups. **(e)** Sex ratio (percentage of males) in each experimental group. All transplanted fish in both conditions developed as phenotypic males, whereas WT controls showed a balanced sex ratio (57% males). These data indicate that transplantation of iPGCs drives sex-biased development and that loss of *tdrd7a* substantially compromises both the fertility and the germline transmission capacity of donor-derived germ cells. Statistical details and raw data are provided in Supplementary Table 1.
